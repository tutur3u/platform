## Overview

The mobile app now ships first-party workspace surfaces for:

- Drive at `Routes.drive`
- CRM at `Routes.crm`
- Education at `Routes.education`

These modules are registered in the Apps hub and available through the mini-app navigation system.

The mobile Apps hub uses the v0.9.2 colored cards with consistent icons and clear descriptions. Keep module ordering explicit in the hub view rather than relying on registry insertion order. For workspace modules that need dense controls, prefer the same shell-owned chrome used by Calendar:

- `ShellMiniNav` for mode or section switching
- `ShellChromeActions` for quick actions such as filters, create, sort, and view toggles
- A compact overview with a small number of relevant metrics
- The v0.9.2 AppCardPalette gradient cards, consistent icon blocks, readable labels, and shared theme colors

The Apps screen defaults to the compact grid. A List/Grid switch in the top
shell header sits beside Customize and changes the same visible, ordered modules
between icon-and-name tiles and descriptive cards. Grid tiles have more space below
the shell header and can be held and dragged to reorder without entering Customize.
Holding an icon enters persistent ordering mode: icons shift during a drag and
continue to wiggle after release until an empty area is tapped. Compact hide
controls sit over icon corners only in grid ordering mode; list cards never show
hide controls. The choice stays in the mounted Apps screen while navigating
within the mobile shell; search and workspace access rules apply to both layouts. Descriptions remain
searchable in grid mode even though they are hidden from the tiles.
Holding a tile produces a pickup haptic, moving onto a new navigation target
produces a selection tick, and completing a drop produces a light confirmation.
The floating dock uses the same semantic feedback. Haptics are enabled by default
on iOS and Android, can be disabled in Preferences, follow system haptic policy,
and throttle repeated feedback during a continuous gesture.
Shell actions and standalone primary actions give a selection tick, and a
speed dial distinguishes opening from choosing an action. Finance transaction
actions give a light completion tap after the local edit is accepted; this tap
does not claim that a queued offline edit has synchronized.

Background warmups for optional products should also respect access state before fetching. Do not let Apps hub or boot-time cache warmups call product endpoints blindly; skip the warmup when the corresponding access cubit is not loaded and enabled, and fail soft on opportunistic `404` responses.

Tasks, Calendar, and Finance are the default launcher modules. Meet, Mail, Chat,
and Notes join the existing opt-in apps in Settings → Experiments; all four are
off until the user enables them. The choice is cached on the device and restored
at launch. Turning a module on exposes it in Apps and its eligible Home card;
turning it off removes those launch surfaces. Mail still requires its account
access rule, and workspace visibility flags still apply. Deep links retain the
existing route access rules. Meet includes meeting search, scheduling, editing,
and deletion when enabled. Every launcher module has an English and Vietnamese
description.

## Navigation preferences and recovery

Phones and tablets share a compact floating icon dock. Leaving a mini-app returns
to its Home or Apps launch screen. Root screens remain mounted with hidden
animations paused, preserving search and scroll state. The brand control opens
the Apps screen within the normal shell, with compact search and arrange actions.
Tapping a dock item glides the inner highlight to the new position without a
button splash. During a hold or drag, the highlight follows the finger freely;
it snaps to the selected item only on release. Reduced-motion settings shorten
these transitions to an immediate state change.
Switching between peer sections in any mini-app replaces the previous section in
navigation history. Back follows the current context's outer layer: a detail
returns to its parent section, a peer section returns to the mini-app root, and
the mini-app root returns to the Home or Apps launch screen. This also applies
to deep links, so Back never visits a sibling section by accident.
Personal Tasks boards are the Tasks entry surface. Their dock and Android system
Back return to the root tab that launched Tasks, rather than the board directory
that automatically opens the default board. Workspace board details retain their
board-directory parent; task overlays return to their owning board.
Apps is always available in the root dock. The Apps grid supports long-press
reordering: nearby icons shift across rows during the drag, with a subtle
motion cue, and the new order is saved on release. Tapping an app's icon or
label exits reorder and opens that app. Empty cell margins, row gaps, and unused
grid space never open an app; tapping them exits reorder. Leaving Apps through
any route also exits reorder, while preserving its search and scroll state.
Long-press pickup and ordinary scrolling retain their existing gesture thresholds.
App taps provide selection haptics. The list editor provides drag handles and visibility controls. Hiding
an app requires confirmation. Hidden apps remain accessible at the bottom of
Apps, separated from shown apps by a divider, and can be shown again without a
confirmation. The editor places hidden experiments below Hidden Apps; enabled
experiments appear in the regular shown order, while disabled or locally hidden
experiments can be enabled or shown from Hidden Experiments. App order and hidden
choices persist on the device. The editor disables Show when current account or
workspace access would still prevent an experiment from appearing; access changes
can make that control available later. Workspace permissions and experimental-app
visibility still take precedence; a locally hidden app is only moved out of
the shown launcher section, not disabled or removed from navigation.

Home's task, calendar, Finance, daily summary, and eligible Mail, Meet, and Notes
cards can be reordered or hidden through the compact top-bar Customize action.
Available cards are shown
in their current order by default, and hidden cards remain in the editor for
recovery.
Unavailable modules do not appear as Home cards or editor choices; their saved
layout preferences remain intact if access returns. The workspace picker and
security prompts remain outside customization.

The shell provides bottom scroll clearance, including for explicit-padding
lists; pages must not add another dock inset. Downward scrolling hides the dock;
reverse scrolling or idle restores it. Profile/Agenda reclaim hidden clearance;
other pages keep it stable. Primary actions register with `ShellActionSpec.inDock`.
`ExtendedFab` uses that registration inside the shell and remains standalone
elsewhere. Actions use icons on phones and labels on tablets. Do not duplicate
these actions in page-level floating buttons or top-bar actions.

Nova spins once when entering Assistant and remains static afterward. Loading
indicators continue animating, while pull-to-refresh uses an opaque circular
surface. Reduced-motion preferences disable spinning.

Calendar forms and event details use readable, blurred root-navigator sheets.
High-contrast mode uses an opaque surface. Failed event
edits or deletions restore the previous in-memory and cached list and expose the
server error. Verify the next reload as well as the immediate rollback.

Dock action handoffs retain presentation only, never callbacks or permission
state. Destination actions remain disabled until their mounted page registers
current handlers; missing destinations discard previews after a short handoff.
Long-press navigation supports freeform dragging between destinations, including
when the pointer leaves the dock. The pill stays under the pointer within the
capsule; crossing the nearest enabled tab gives selection feedback, and release
snaps to and selects that tab. The floating surface blurs content behind it,
uses one translucent outline, and contains the moving glow inside the capsule.
Pressing gently scales the island and smoothly brightens that internal glow,
which fades on release. Dock controls use accessibility labels without hover
tooltips. The Profile dock reserves its avatar space with a default placeholder,
shows a skeleton while an image downloads, and displays the cached image when
ready. Back from a peer navigation section exits to
the root tab that opened that context; back from a detail page returns to its
parent section. Shell search actions across Apps, Chat, Finance, Habits, Mail,
Meet, Notes, task boards, and Timer focus the top search input on entry. The
search icon, input, and close control share one vertical center; submitting an
empty or whitespace-only query closes search mode except in Apps, which keeps
its current search. Chat shows message matches
in the page, while task boards and Timer keep filtering their current lists.

The App, Workspace, and You dock destinations consolidate into one Settings
screen at `/settings`, with Workspace and You as nested navigation rows. App
preferences use compact grouped items, retaining two columns at widths of at
least 840dp. Root rows
show the current value and open nested choices; Finance visibility, haptics, and
Task board behavior never switch directly from a root tap. Opening, canceling,
dismissing, or pressing Back in a child does not write a preference. An explicit
choice is required, and an account/workspace switch cancels a pending editor,
including a switch away and back. This replaces the earlier inline-root-toggle
design. Existing theme, language, calendar, finance, and haptics sources remain
the owners; personal timezone still overrides workspace, then device timezone.
Timezone loading, unknown, retry, same-scope failure retention, and workspace
permission states stay visible. Permission reloads fail closed and stale results
cannot expose an editor or administrative destination.

Production native timezone reads use the Infrastructure Calendar gateway. Verified
Bearer sessions use the same hashed session trust key as server authentication;
header presence alone never grants a trusted bucket. A 429 response retains its
`Retry-After` cooldown across workspace changes for the same account. Settings
show the wait state and suppress repeated reads until that time; the previous
account's cooldown and preferences do not apply to a different account. A successfully read personal or
workspace preference stays visible if the other read fails, while effective
timezone can remain unresolved; an independently loaded preference remains
editable after any 429 cooldown ends, while the failed read retains its retry
state.
Diagnosis must distinguish gateway status, preferences reads and native plugin
resolution; an Unknown label alone does not establish which failed.
Concurrent loads for the same account and workspace share one pending resolution.
The repository also shares pending personal reads across that account's workspaces,
while workspace reads and all account data stay separate. Completed reads are not
cached by this mechanism; the next explicit refresh reads again. After a pending
read's existing load deadline expires, a retry starts a new read; late transport
results cannot overwrite it. A resume refresh for the same scope does not
invalidate an in-flight preference save. Any 429 without
a positive parsed `Retry-After` uses a conservative 30-second cooldown. A failed
save keeps its requested value for an explicit retry after the cooldown; no timer
automatically retries reads or writes. Shared repository callers honor the account's
cooldown, and late results cannot overwrite the current account or workspace.
`timezone_load_coalescing_test.dart` and `timezone_gateway_retry_test.dart` cover
these contracts, including HTTP-date retry headers and partial preference recovery.
The Infrastructure Calendar gateway forwards only bounded scalar rate-policy,
caller-class, window, limit, remaining, reset, block-reason and Turnstile challenge
headers plus `Retry-After`. Cookies, credentials, redirects and arbitrary upstream
debug headers are excluded; JSON responses remain private and uncached. Gateway
tests cover the allowlist and malformed values. These diagnostics help identify a
blocking layer; source checks do not identify the cause of a production 429.

Settings choice sheets scroll the heading and choices together in one bounded
viewport, so a long heading at large text sizes cannot consume the entire choice
area. Cancel stays outside that viewport. Other non-sliver dialog callers retain
their fixed heading by default; timezone choices keep their existing lazy slivers.
Mounted compact navigation coverage verifies visible choice space, real Finance
scrolling, dismissal without a write, and isolation from the underlying dock.

The app-wide Home/Apps dock is unchanged. Workspace is a child of Settings, so
Back returns to Settings; deeper workspace pages return to their parent. You opens
the existing Profile, preserving its navigation and account behavior. Legacy
Settings URLs and the Preferences redirect remain supported. Session, About, and
open-source licenses remain accessible. Root rows use existing theme tokens,
quiet shared borders, concise labels, muted values, and at least 48dp touch targets;
they grow with large text. Contextual Time Tracker, Mail, Meet, and Assistant

Settings tiles center a single title with the icon. For tiles with a value or
supporting line, the first title line aligns with the icon. Workspace settings
use the shell's Workspace title and omit a second in-content heading. Settings
subpages likewise use the shell title once, without a repeated introduction.
Finance and Inventory read the workspace currency from the encrypted local
snapshot before their first frame. Inventory product and sales lists likewise
show cached rows and periods while background revalidation runs; a cold workspace

Notification Inbox and Archive actions share a bordered segmented control with
primary selected colors and accessibility selection state. Use `segmentGroup`

Mobile notifications use `App: item title` as the title and put the event detail

Security approval pushes use `Security` while retaining their challenge data

Mail owns folder, mailbox, and filter selectors through `ShellMiniNav`. Opening

Chat bubbles expose copy through long press or secondary click. Markdown in these
bubbles must not install a competing native text-selection long-press recognizer.
Other standalone markdown views remain selectable.

## Responsive Loading And Cache Verification

### Device task and calendar reminders

Settings → Notifications & interaction → Reminders controls task and calendar alerts separately.
Both default on, with selectable 3-day, 1-day, 12-hour, 3-hour, and 1-hour
lead times. The app refreshes the existing user/workspace-scoped task and
calendar caches after sign-in and when it resumes after 15 minutes. It then
schedules the nearest 60 future device alerts, replacing outdated entries and
cancelling them on sign-out. An all-day event uses 9 a.m. local time as its
reminder anchor. Android boot/package-replacement receivers restore pending
alarms after a restart or update.

Calendar alerts use a localized timing title (for example, “Calendar: In 3
days”) and the event name as the description, including for all-day events.
Tapping one opens that event in the mobile Calendar detail sheet. If the event
was removed or cannot be loaded, the app returns to Calendar and shows a brief
message. This behavior applies to device-scheduled reminders on iOS and Android;
task reminders keep their existing title and description.

The Reminders page shows the last successful fetch, the number of alerts still
pending in the operating system, the next scheduled alert, notification
permission, and a manual refresh action. A scheduled count is local state,
not proof that the device displayed an alert. If the app remains suspended,
new items created on another device cannot enter the local schedule until the
next app refresh. The deployed web cron checks watched task deadlines every five
minutes across workspaces, queries only the active reminder windows, and creates
notifications through each user's channel preferences, including mobile push.
Workspace task reminder settings can change or disable the lead times; without
an override, the server uses the five device
defaults. Its receipt includes the due date, so a rescheduled task is eligible
again. Calendar event reminders still depend on the device schedule until the
server calendar reminder flow is shipped.
Verify this flow on physical iOS and Android devices with the app closed,
notifications enabled and disabled, account switching, changed due dates, and
completed tasks.

Invalidation and logout prevent older
in-flight requests from writing back into the cache. Keys must include user,
workspace, and all query filters.

Offline refresh consistency (cache-backed reads in all mobile products): a
two-phase read and its
nested repository reads share one refresh cycle. Reconnect first revalidates
visited resources, then visible projections reuse those completed reads. A later
explicit refresh starts a new cycle; scope changes and invalidation fence earlier
results. Explicit setup/catalog refreshes report transport failure; ordinary
offline reads retain confirmed rows and project queued edits. Checkout coalesces overlapping quote reads, while an explicit retry after
a settled request remains available. Removing one retained resource invalidates
only the corresponding downloaded product for its actor and workspace.
Each product must retain its shared permission snapshot before download readiness
is accepted; Finance also requires retained exchange rates. Eviction of either
dependency prevents readiness even when the product's own catalog is complete.

Snapshot replacement, source indexes, authoritative download reconciliation,
and pressure eviction share one resource ordering boundary. An encrypted durable
journal restores the previous completed snapshot after interrupted publication
or cleanup. A persisted clear intent takes priority over rollback on restart;
logout, actor changes and explicit invalidation must not resurrect old data.
Resource-only clearing preserves unsynchronized outbox edits and local-ID mapping
provenance. Replica extraction accepts known response envelopes, not arbitrary
nested relation or metadata objects. Canonical domain fields survive sparse
responses, while explicit clearing values stay cleared. Regression evidence:
`cache_store_publication_test.dart` and `cache_replica_policy_test.dart`.

Connectivity fallback is restricted to typed transport failures (socket, timeout,
or HTTP-client transport errors). Invalid JSON, unexpected response shapes,
oversized bodies, and failed session refreshes remain visible failures rather
than presenting a cached response as a successful refresh. Download pacing wait
is outside the actual HTTP request timeout. Search patterns are compiled once per
retained search pattern rather than once per candidate row.
Mutation queue admission uses the same typed transport rule. The explicit
`OFFLINE_CONTRACT_UNAVAILABLE` exception is admitted only for replay-safe writes;
session, malformed-response, and unknown failures never become pending writes.

Reopened sale details immediately project queued sales-period assignments,
including explicit removal, over the scoped confirmed snapshot. They retain
queued status and local IDs until acknowledgment; pending IDs never become server
mutation targets.
Definitive not-found responses, including a locally queued deletion, clear
retained sale details and mutation controls. Transient refresh failures retain
the authorized snapshot. Regression evidence: `offline_mutation_admission_test.dart`,
`cache_revalidation_test.dart`,
`offline_download_manifest_test.dart`, `offline_preparation_coordinator_test.dart`,
`api_client_transport_failure_test.dart`, `inventory_sale_period_projection_test.dart`,
`inventory_sale_detail_refresh_test.dart`, and `season_pricing_controller_test.dart`
under `apps/mobile/test`.

Failed disk writes do not hide successful network responses. Mail and Meet retain
the original authorization error and disable their cache if denied-data cleanup fails.
Keys longer than Hive's 255-byte UTF-8 limit use a SHA-256 digest of the complete
scoped identity. Keep short keys stable, and test persistence/reopening with real
UUIDs and long search filters; short mock IDs can hide storage failures.

Mail list snapshots use the existing encrypted Hive store, with a one-minute
freshness window and a seven-day retention window. Cached rows appear while a
background refresh runs, including after their freshness window. A background
authorization denial revokes the affected workspace snapshot, clears the inbox,
and closes an open reader. Pull-to-refresh forces a network read, and successful mutations
invalidate the workspace's Mail variants. Thread details use the same scoped
cache; permissions and individual messages (including editable drafts) are
fetched from the server. General attachment bytes, credentials, and active compose buffers are
not cached. Authorization failures discard the displayed list. Search waits 300ms
after typing, and list rows are built lazily. Folder metadata loads independently
so it cannot hold up the inbox.
Small inline images keep an encrypted thread-scoped copy. A cached image renders
immediately while one shared background request refreshes it; a denied image
request revokes the affected Mail workspace and clears its thread media.

Inventory can synchronously restore an unexpired user/workspace-scoped snapshot
before currency and permission requests finish. Editing remains disabled until
current permissions are confirmed. A denied request removes displayed content;
cached data must never grant mutation permissions.

### Native API requests and hosting challenges

An HTTP 429 HTML response with `x-vercel-mitigated: challenge` is a Vercel browser
challenge, not the application's JSON rate-limit response. Native HTTP clients
cannot complete that challenge.

Production mobile Calendar requests use the infrastructure app's
`/api/v1/mobile-calendar/api/v1/…` gateway. Development uses the local Calendar
app directly. The gateway validates the Bearer session with Supabase before
reading any forwarding credential. It forwards only event CRUD, connected-account
list/disconnect, calendar connection list/toggle, and OAuth URL discovery to the
fixed `https://calendar.tuturuuu.com` origin. Calendar validates the original
session again and enforces workspace permissions. Incoming cookies, forwarding
headers, and client-supplied gateway credentials are never relayed. Redirects and
non-JSON upstream responses are rejected; mutations are never automatically retried.

The server credential is stored as a versioned encrypted envelope in the root
workspace's `workspace_secrets` row named `mobile_calendar_gateway:v1`. Its data
key is encrypted with the infrastructure app's existing `ENCRYPTION_MASTER_KEY`.
The envelope contains `version: 1`, `encryptedDataKey`, and `encryptedSecret`.
Only the server decrypts it; it must never enter mobile build configuration,
logs, source control, or the signing bundle.

Configure Calendar's custom WAF exception to require both its API path and an
exact `x-tuturuuu-calendar-gateway` header value matching that private credential.
Do not accept the mere presence of an Authorization header as a WAF bypass.
The private credential allows verified gateway traffic through hosting challenges;
it does not replace Calendar's application authentication or authorization.

For cutover, deploy and verify the gateway, then publish clients using it. Replace
the old path-plus-Bearer exception with the server-credential rule once both store
builds are available. Verify invalid sessions return JSON 401 from the gateway
without an upstream request, a signed-in Calendar loads real events, and direct
Calendar requests with a forged Bearer or gateway header still receive the hosting
challenge. Older clients that call Calendar directly need the mobile update.
Keep publication on hold if gateway verification fails. Inspect the exact project's
active rules and unpublished draft before changing or publishing its WAF config.

For credential rotation, create a new encrypted envelope, temporarily allow both
old and new **server credentials** at the WAF, update the secret row, verify a live
Calendar read, then remove the old credential. Never restore the public Bearer
exception as a fallback. Missing or invalid secret configuration fails closed with
JSON 503. Responses use `private, no-store`; request bodies are limited to 512 KiB,
response bodies to 8 MiB, and forwarding to 20 seconds.

## Locked Notes

Mobile Notes defaults to a device-first encrypted lock. Where OS local
authentication is available, Face ID or Touch ID gates a random note key in
device-only secure storage; otherwise the lock sheet defaults to a six-digit
app PIN. The lock encrypts the note body; the title and list metadata retain
their existing visibility. The PIN gates that local key and is never used as
the encryption password. Five incorrect PIN attempts pause retries for 30
seconds. The server
also stores a wrapped copy of the random key in the note envelope using the
configured encryption master key. Creating a device lock fails if wrapping is
unavailable. Web releases that key only for the note creator after a verified
passkey sign-in within five minutes, on web or mobile. Account sign-in alone
does not release it. Native passkeys use `webcredentials:tuturuuu.com` on iOS
and the `get_login_creds` Digital Asset Links relation for the production
Android signing certificate. Keep the association files and mobile signing
identities aligned when changing app IDs or certificates.
The user can also show a two-minute QR code on web and scan it from the
original phone's note actions; the phone requires fresh local unlock and sends
the key encrypted with a one-time QR secret. The QR secret stays on the two
devices. The server relays only the sealed key. A custom passphrase remains
available and existing passphrase-locked notes keep their current behavior.
Server-wrapped recovery means this lock is not end-to-end encrypted against the
server. Preserve the configured encryption master key when rotating deployment
secrets; losing it disables passkey recovery for existing locked notes, though
the original phone or its QR transfer can still open them. On compact layouts,
Back closes any open Notes action sheet and returns
from note detail to the Notes list.

### Device authenticators and trusted registrations

Home suggests registering the current device when local authentication is
available, the account has no verified authenticator on that device, and new
registrations are unlocked. “Not now” suppresses this invitation for seven days
per account. A failed policy lookup never produces a misleading invitation.
Settings > Session always provides the explicit management entry point.

Registration creates a Supabase TOTP factor and a separate random device proof.
The mobile app saves both in account-scoped secure storage before verifying the
factor. iOS uses a non-synchronizing, this-device-only Keychain item. Each code
reveal, approval and management change requires fresh OS authentication; PIN or
passcode fallback is allowed. Codes disappear when the app backgrounds.

`/api/v1/auth/mfa/devices` uses request-scoped authentication for web cookies and
mobile Bearer tokens. Server-owned `app_metadata.tuturuuu_device_authenticators`
contains the registration policy and proof hashes, never TOTP secrets. The
registry API serializes writes with a bounded Upstash lease and fails closed if
`UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN` is unavailable. Do not log
registration response bodies, proofs, TOTP secrets, or session tokens.

The trusted-device list supports revocation and locking new registrations.
Enabling the lock requires two currently verified trusted factors. Unlocking
requires a current trusted-device proof and AAL2; the last trusted device cannot
remove itself while locked. Keep a backup trusted device: there is no automatic
lost-all-devices recovery bypass. The lock governs Tuturuuu device registrations;
it does not disable third-party enrollment through Supabase's own MFA APIs.
Only canonical, verified registry entries with valid proofs can approve Tuturuuu
sign-ins. A client-supplied device identifier or editable profile cannot grant
trust. Provider-level MFA enrollment can invalidate other sessions, so setup
explains that other devices may need to sign in again.

### QR and number-matching approvals

The scanner at `Routes.settingsQrLoginScan` accepts Tuturuuu QR payloads from
allowlisted origins; manual link entry supports devices without a camera.
Approval requires fresh local authentication, a trusted-device proof and a
verified mobile MFA session. The server checks the challenge secret, origin,
protocol kind, expiry and one-time consumption. QR handoff creates a new session;
Supabase MFA requirements still apply to that new session.

The web MFA screen creates a session-bound
`/api/v1/auth/mfa/mobile/challenges` request and shows a six-digit number.
The mobile user must enter that number. The pending-approval API never returns
it to the approving device. Approvals are rate-limited, expire after their short
challenge lifetime, and cannot be approved by the requesting session itself.
Users can explicitly deny an unexpected request.

`MobileMfaApprovalListener` polls while authenticated, foregrounded and unlocked.
Push receipt triggers an immediate refresh. A notification contains only account
and challenge identifiers plus expiry, never a code or credential. Tapping it
opens a page that re-fetches the authorized request. Notifications do not approve
from the lock screen. Polling remains the fallback when notification permission
or provider delivery is unavailable.

After consumption, the web poller sets the HTTP-only
`ttr_mfa_mobile_approval` cookie, bound to the same user, web session, hidden
challenge secret and unexpired canonical approval. This is an application-level
web approval; it does not mint a Supabase AAL2 token. Mobile AAL2 still comes from
Supabase MFA verification.

### Sessions and account connections

Settings > Session > Devices and connections lists active sessions and linked
sign-in identities. Users can revoke another session, revoke other sessions,
connect a supported OAuth provider, or disconnect an identity while retaining
another sign-in method. Destructive actions require confirmation and local
verification. Account changes during an asynchronous operation fail closed.

Multi-account persistence snapshots the current session after profile loading,
so a refresh during that request cannot save an older token. Authenticated API
retries are account-scoped and refuse to replay after an account switch.

- Multi-account removal and all-account logout must treat Supabase
  `auth.signOut()` as required before clearing the local account store or
  emitting an unauthenticated Cubit state. Google/provider logout and stored
  refresh-token revocation can be best-effort, but a failed Supabase logout must
  leave the account visible with an error so the persisted session cannot
  silently reappear later.
- Auth account models may serialize refresh tokens and session JSON only for the
  encrypted secure-storage store. Do not include those raw secrets in
  `Equatable.props`, `toString`, or other Cubit/Bloc state debug output because
  the global Bloc observer logs state changes during development.

## Offline preparation and synchronization

Finance, Inventory, Tasks, and Calendar keep visited responses in the encrypted,
user/workspace-scoped cache. Reopening uses local snapshots immediately while
revalidating. Loaded list pages and detail responses also materialize local rows;
searching a new query offline does not require that exact query to have been
requested online. Reconnect, app resume, and refreshed authentication replay the
outbox before refreshing visited resources. Visible lists reload after replay.
Only one replay runs at a time. Cached screens publish their first snapshot,
then await fresh data without starting a second cold fetch or a cache-notifier
loop. Transport failures retain the original scoped snapshot and its timestamp.
Settings and pending rows distinguish queued,
syncing, failed, and conflicting writes. An uncertain money operation stays in
manual review unless its endpoint guarantees idempotency; replay must never turn
a cache refresh failure after acknowledgment into a second write.

Settings → Offline → Download all is an explicit download for the current
account and workspace. Products settle sequentially, with per-product state,
historical completion time, and targeted retry. Inventory downloads catalog pages
and each product detail; sales, audit, and finance transaction history are paged.
Audit pages are indexed by their server audit record IDs and reused across UI
page sizes, ordered by occurrence time descending.
Finance also prepares checkpoint summaries, wallet-specific statistics, and
account-scoped global exchange rates, so these reads work on a first offline visit.
Tasks include board collections, task details, relationships, and supported
history. Calendar cursor pages cover the API-supported year range 0001–9999 and
connection metadata. Its paginated requests cap each page at 500 rows; normal
unpaginated requests accept at most 400 days. Pagination has finite safety bounds and fails rather than silently
claiming completion when a cursor does not advance. Wallet checkpoint preparation
currently follows the mobile endpoint's latest-50 window; it does not promise a
lifetime checkpoint archive.

Completed full collections reconcile obsolete list/detail snapshots so removed
server rows do not survive in local search. Failed downloads retain older
snapshots and pending writes. Each explicit download requires successful
persistent writes and a final check
that its requested snapshots survived storage limits. A full run checks earlier
products again for eviction. A restored completion timestamp is historical and
shows **Needs refresh**, never proof that all rows remain stored. Cache eviction, clearing cache, changing quota, or changing account/workspace
invalidates readiness. A scope
switch stops subsequent requests; an already-started request settles before
another bulk run starts. Normal visits fill a bounded older history window with
15-minute throttling, rather than rerunning Download all automatically.

Bulk GETs are globally serialized on the device and spaced by at least 750 ms at the request boundary, using a monotonic clock after queued authentication preparation. Early timer wakeups are rechecked; regression coverage lives in `offline_api_pacer_test.dart` and `api_client_offline_preparation_test.dart`. This stays below the hourly session budget even
when the gateway and owning app both enforce the guard. Ordinary unmarked
foreground reads do not consume these additional bulk-download budgets.
The additional server download protection applies only to eligible GET/HEAD requests explicitly marked `x-tuturuuu-offline-download: 1`. Ordinary daily enterprise operations in Finance, Inventory, Contacts, Tasks and other apps always continue through existing authentication, authorization, MFA, abuse and API protections without accessing this additional guard's Redis clients, quotas or CAPTCHA, even when it is activated. This scope applies to the web API and every registered satellite proxy. `OFFLINE_DOWNLOAD_PROTECTION_ENABLED` defaults to inactive and activates only for the exact value `true`; while inactive, marked bulk downloads return 503 with `Retry-After: 30`. Omitting the bulk marker never bypasses baseline protections or grants access.

Before enabling offline prefetch, provision `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` and `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_TOKEN` on every gateway and owning-app replica that can handle these reads. Verify distributed limiter operations, shared clearance storage, Turnstile configuration, and authenticated ordinary-read and marked-bulk canaries on the exact deployment. Enable the activation flag only after those checks pass, consistently across the request path; available Redis or elevated account trust never implicitly activates the feature. Keep the flag inactive during initial provisioning. Once activated, Redis/configuration/runtime failures still return 503; there is no automatic outage bypass or process-local substitute.

When activated, the shared proxy enforces explicitly marked eligible bulk reads: 1,200/minute and 30,000/hour per IP, plus 600/minute and 12,000/hour per session when present. Gateway and owning-app enforcement may both count a marked request. Bulk requests require Turnstile clearance unless the server has verified elevated trust; clearance lasts 15 minutes and never bypasses authentication, permissions, MFA, blocks, or quotas. Ordinary unmarked requests do not spend these additional offline-download allowances.
Regular reconnect refreshes are paced without the bulk marker; optional history fills use silent bulk mode. Background work never opens a verification modal and pauses when challenged. Verification-required 403 responses preserve existing authorized snapshots; authorization-denied responses purge the denied resource. A 429 stops downloads and delays queued replay according to Retry-After, with at least a minute's wait.
Regression coverage in [offline-download-guard.test.ts](https://github.com/tutur3u/platform/blob/main/packages/utils/src/__tests__/offline-download-guard.test.ts) proves unmarked ordinary reads avoid Redis, offline quotas and CAPTCHA with activation on or off, inactive bulk remains blocked, and activated bulk storage failures, quotas and CAPTCHA remain enforced.

Local Inventory search mirrors name-only SQL ILIKE, trimmed whole query, case
insensitivity, preserved accents, `%`/`_` wildcards, and backslash escaping.
Catalog ordering is creation time descending, then stable ID. Finance matches the
untrimmed query against descriptions and orders by taken time, then creation time,
both descending. Cached `[CONFIDENTIAL]` descriptions are not searchable: offline
search cannot reconstruct hidden server fields or newly granted permissions.
The cache represents the last authorized download; changes made elsewhere,
permission revocations, and authoritative stock/balance calculations require a
server refresh. Sales can be drafted offline from cached catalog and season
prices, but remain queued until the server validates price IDs and commits them.
Mobile applies configured current-period, revenue-wallet, and finance-category
defaults while preserving explicit choices. Legacy period sales also use the
atomic invoice request with a stable request ID; period rejection must not leave
an already-created invoice. Reconcile cart refreshes current metadata/prices and
removes incompatible or unavailable lines, then asks the operator to review the
total. It applies only to the editable current cart, never historical sales or a
frozen uncertain checkout journal.
Stock health remains the last confirmed snapshot and excludes local drafts.
Workspace permissions are refreshed online and persisted as an encrypted
account/workspace-scoped UI hint. A missing network interface or transport failure
may reuse that hint; authorization failures remove it, and switching accounts cannot reuse grants.
Download all also prepares this metadata. Local permissions never authorize
server writes: the server checks current permissions when replaying each mutation.
Product images are cached as encrypted bytes when displayed and during Inventory
Download all. Cards, checkout rows, and editor previews use the same scoped cache.
Image bytes share the storage quota and clearing/eviction behavior with product
records. Downloads accept HTTPS media with at most three HTTPS redirects, never
send app credentials, and enforce an 8 MiB file and 16 megapixel image limit.
A required image that cannot be retained makes the product download fail rather
than claiming full offline readiness. Cache keys digest image URLs so signed
read tokens are not exposed in plaintext keys.

Offline inventory acceptance is scoped to the current account and workspace. A queued checkout receives a local pending identifier, never a committed invoice receipt; pending local catalog/setup edits must synchronize before checkout. Read-through helpers retain their default compatibility behavior while pinning an available account at entry, checking it at request and cache publication boundaries, and rejecting late responses after account changes. Request generations also prevent older workspace loads from replacing the active screen.

For marked bulk reads, the authenticated gateway uses a session budget when the client IP is unknown; an anonymous marked request without an attributable IP fails closed. Known-IP bulk requests retain their IP budget. These controls do not grant offline clients server authorization. Product images share one 60-second deadline across redirects and body streaming, and unrelated resource eviction does not cancel their fetch.

Deferred cache transaction/refresh sharing, transport draining, search performance, and cosmetic improvements are tracked in [#5737](https://github.com/tutur3u/platform/issues/5737). App disposal invalidates preparation readiness immediately; an already-started read may fail when its HTTP client closes. Graceful draining remains a followup and does not permit a disposed preparation to publish readiness or replay writes.
