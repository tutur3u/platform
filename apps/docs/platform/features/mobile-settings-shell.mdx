## Settings row grid

Settings sections and detail rows share `SettingsRowLayout`. Muted captions sit
4px inside a rounded (24px) grouped card. Rows use a 16px inset, a 24px slot for
a thin Lucide line icon, a 14px gap before a medium-weight (w500) title and a
44px trailing action column with a 17px Lucide chevron. Settings and Appearance
use Lucide icons only. Inset dividers start at the title column (54px), not under
the icon. Rows have a 56px minimum height and expand for translated or enlarged
text. A row shows its current value as a muted summary under the title, or as a
short trailing value before the chevron (for example, the Appearance theme name).
It does not show both, except the Time zone rows below. A trailing value keeps its natural width, flush right,
while the title still fits on one line; otherwise it moves under the title, so
titles never break mid-word at enlarged text. Full-size controls such as
switches widen the trailing column and centre vertically instead of scaling
down. Multiple timezone actions
stack in the trailing column, keeping the text width and action centers
consistent with chevrons. Existing English and Vietnamese labels and tooltips
remain unchanged.

Settings scroll lists use the design foundation's tokens (`AppLayout`): content
starts 8px below the shell header (`pageTop`) and ends with the shell-provided
dock clearance plus a 24px end gap (`scrollEndGap`). Row height (56px), row inset
(16px) and the gap between groups (24px) are the foundation's row and section
tokens. They inherit the shell surface and keep Back in its floating dock. These presentation defaults do not change timezone reads, writes, retries
or permissions. `settings_design_checks.dart` covers row geometry, action touch
targets and the final control clearing the dock; the compact navigation suite
also exercises enlarged text in both languages and themes.

### Time zone rows

Each Time zone row shows one value line: the zone as a city with its current
GMT offset, for example "Ho Chi Minh City (GMT+7)". Offsets follow daylight saving
time, and the offset stays attached to its city. Raw identifiers such as
`Asia/Saigon` are never shown, so they can't break mid-word. Legacy device ids
map to their current city, and Vietnamese shows "TP. Hồ Chí Minh". When the
personal choice is Automatic, the row shows "Auto" on its title line and names
the zone that applies. An explicit choice shows only that zone. The workspace row
shows only the workspace choice, so the effective zone is never repeated.
Loading and account states replace the value rather than adding lines. Errors and
rate limits keep their wrapped explanation and Retry action.

These rows are the one place a row shows both a value line and a short trailing
value. When a row has a value line, its trailing value sits on the title line, so
the value keeps the full text width; at enlarged text it moves under the title.
The explanation of Automatic and the zone list stay in the picker opened from the
row. Offsets resolve through `core/utils/timezone_location.dart`, which loads the
full tz data when a native alias such as `Asia/Saigon` is missing.
`timezone_label_test.dart` checks every supported zone,
`settings_timezone_rows_checks.dart` checks the one-line rows in both languages,
and `timezone_resolution_scope_test.dart` checks the scoped states.

## Settings detail shell and profile settings

All mobile Settings children use the same shell background and one shell top
navbar with the destination title and actions. In-app Back appears only in the
floating bottom navbar, never in the top navbar. Android system Back remains
available. Offline is `/settings/offline`; its
Finance, Inventory, Tasks and Calendar details are `/settings/offline/<module>`.
They do not add body title/back bars, nested AppBars or another top safe area.
Their scroll content reserves the shell-provided bottom padding, so the floating
dock clears the last actionable row. Pushed destinations return to the screen
that opened them; direct links fall back to their Settings parent.

Settings → Edit profile, opened from the Settings profile header, is the one
place for account and profile settings at `/settings/profile`: Identity (display
name, full name, email with any pending change), Avatar (Photo, Remove avatar
and a Cover banner row that adds, changes or removes the banner), Account (Manage
accounts at `/settings/accounts`, Sessions and devices) and Account status, as
captioned grouped cards with the shell title "Edit profile". Its identity card is
borderless with a circular avatar, matching the Settings header, and an unknown
join date is omitted. Manage accounts appears only here; the root has neither a
You row nor a Manage accounts row, and its group caption is General. Legacy
`/profile/edit` and `/profile/accounts` links redirect to those destinations.
`/profile` retains the Overview/Timeline activity surface. Its edit entry is a
compact icon with an accessible Identity label and tooltip. Account-switching
shortcuts remain available there. These defaults apply in both mobile languages;
workspace permissions and queued profile writes retain their existing rules.

Profile identity fields use the shared Settings tiles in grouped cards, with
read-only values until a field is opened for editing. Manage accounts uses the
same grouped Add account row and account panels; its shell title is not repeated
in a hero card. The Profile account-management entry has one label, and What's
new keeps its description and installed version without repeating the shell
heading. Account removal confirmations and queued profile writes are unchanged.
These defaults apply to both languages, light/dark themes and enlarged text.

Avatar removal in Settings → Edit profile captures the authenticated account when its
confirmation opens. An account switch or logout invalidates that confirmation,
even if the original account returns before confirmation. Removal uses the
existing avatar DELETE and offline queue contracts, with the original actor
bound through queue admission and HTTP dispatch. Denied removals show localized
media recovery; only an explicit Remove avatar action retries. A confirmed
removal reloads the profile and persists the returned avatar state. Banner
upload/removal and storage policy retain their existing contracts.

`profile_avatar_removal_test.dart` covers confirmation lifetime, cleared-avatar
reload/persistence, and EN/VI recovery. `profile_media_actor_race_test.dart`
covers queue/cache actor changes and HTTP failures without automatic retry.
These tests use synthetic actors and storage; authenticated device and real
storage verification remain separate gates.

Licenses, onboarding replay and explicit admin edit forms intentionally open
above the shell on the root navigator with their own full-screen chrome. Sheet
editors retain their existing modal presentation. They never stack a second
navbar within a shell body.

Home Agenda, Profile Overview/Timeline and Calendar Agenda reclaim the dock's
bottom scroll clearance while it is hidden, retaining the physical safe area.
The viewport remains full height; reverse scrolling or idle restores the dock
and its clearance. Other screens keep stable clearance by default. Text Assistant
publishes a lifecycle-scoped composer/action slot into the same shell-owned
floating dock used by every root and detail destination. The material stays
mounted as navigation and prompt content change; width and root bodies animate
independently, with only the current dock controls interactive. No page mounts a
second dock. Reduced motion disables transitions, and a focused composer remains
visible above the keyboard. Draft controllers stay with the cached Assistant
page, while disposed publishers cannot overwrite a newer registration. Text
immersive mode retains the same composer and navigation toggle,
including when entered through an Assistant tool. Live keeps normal navigation while idle or connecting; only connected call
controls replace it, retaining the navbar width and the same shell material. While no workspace is resolved, shell
navigation remains available.

Shell profile reads and cache mutations are fenced by current actor and request
generation. Account changes, logout and newer edits reject late results; cache
writes and clearing remain ordered. Feature-level identity guards also remain.

Regression coverage: `settings_detail_shell_checks.dart` exercises actual
Offline registrations, inherited background/header and dock clearance, enlarged
text, system/dock back without a top Back button, and unknown module links;
`profile_overview_shell_test.dart` covers the icon edit entry and return path.
`floating_shell_dock_test.dart` verifies hidden clearance and stable viewport;
`shell_assistant_composer_test.dart` verifies one dock, draft/focus and Live
transitions, and `shell_profile_races_test.dart` covers delayed account/cache

### Centered navigation and action group

Root and mini-app destinations center the combined visible navigation island and
its actions whenever they fit. Empty action columns do not consume space after
exit. Dynamic actions reserve width first, moving navigation left, then fade and scale into the free space. On
exit, controls disappear before their width is released and navigation recenters.
The combined navigation/action group stays centered at every intermediate frame;
actions do not animate through a size clip. An interrupted transition reverses
from its current geometry, and a replaced outgoing action cannot accept input or
accessibility focus. Reduced motion settles both phases immediately. Actions
keep 52px slots and share the bottom baseline, including when large text makes
the composer taller; narrow layouts keep secondary actions in the top navbar.
When the viewport becomes too narrow, the second dock column is removed
immediately rather than retaining it while shrinking navigation touch targets. The navigation and Assistant dock share horizontal and safe-area bottom insets, and
switching root destinations retains one dock while bodies ease between tabs.
Only the active body receives pointer input or semantics. Visited tabs remain
mounted, preserving drafts and scroll positions; reduced-motion changes retain
the same page elements. Navigation and prompt slots never paint two docks.
Coverage: `dock_action_choreography_test.dart`, `persistent_shell_dock_test.dart`,
`lazy_indexed_stack_test.dart`, `floating_dock_rail_test.dart`,
`apps_search_submit_test.dart`, and `shell_assistant_composer_test.dart`.

Leaving Apps or launching a module clears Apps search so returning starts in
navigation mode. Workspace picker search replaces the shell title, and its
visible footer actions are centered. Hiding and restoring workspace shortcuts
belongs to Settings; the normal picker never exposes hide buttons. Coverage:
`workspace_picker_sheet_test.dart`. Native dark launch backgrounds match the
actual app theme, checked by `native_splash_theme_test.dart`; the locked screen
shows only the lock status and unlock action. Device visual, transition, and
keyboard checks on the exact CI-built artifact remain required.

## Notification navigation and refresh

Mobile Notifications keeps its scoped snapshot visible while revalidating on
entry. Concurrent unread badge refreshes share one awaitable request; the visible
feed owns its push listener, and count-only changes do not rebuild page rows.
Unchanged counts do not serialize the retained feed again. Workspace switches
clear the visible feed and reload the selected tab; responses from an older
workspace or actor cannot replace current reads. Pagination and offline pending
read overlays retain their existing behavior. These defaults apply to the native
Inbox and Archive; the sheet also updates its unread header. Empty and error
messages use a minimum height rather than a fixed height, so narrow screens and
enlarged text can wrap without clipping. The real 320px root transition matrix
in `shell_assistant_composer_test.dart` covers this responsive empty-state path.

`notifications_cubit_test.dart` covers coalescing, retry, delayed scope responses
and close safety. Focused tests establish request behavior, not a measured device
frame-time improvement.

The persistent notifications action button coalesces unread refreshes only for
the same signed-in actor and workspace. An account transition clears the badge,
retained feeds and pending state before starting a new request, including a
switch away and back in the same workspace. Delayed old requests cannot replace
the new count or end its loading state. This applies to the native shell action
button and shared notifications cubit; delayed-actor and same-workspace ABA
regressions are in `notifications_cubit_test.dart`.

## Settings > Apps: one home for app settings

Every native app's settings live in Settings under a captioned **Apps** group.
Each row shows a summary of current values and opens that app's settings. This
applies to both mobile languages, all themes and enlarged text. Each setting
has exactly one editor and keeps its existing storage, scope cancellation and
permission gate. Rows appear only when the app is available. Timer appears when
its opt-in experiment is enabled. Mail requires mail discovery and a workspace.
Assistant requires a signed-in account and a workspace. The registry does not
grant permissions or invent settings.

| Setting | Previous surface | Central location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open Tasks board by default | Settings root, offline Tasks page | Apps → Tasks (`/settings/apps/tasks`) |
| First day of week | Settings root, Calendar product dialog | Apps → Calendar (`/settings/apps/calendar`) |
| Calendar accounts and visibility | Calendar top-bar action only | Apps → Calendar; the action remains |
| Finance amounts | Settings root, offline Finance page, product dialog | Apps → Finance (`/settings/apps/finance`); the eye quick toggle remains |
| Pomodoro lengths and auto-start | Timer gear sheet | Apps → Timer → Pomodoro (`/settings/apps/timer`) |
| Missed-entry approval threshold and grace period | Timer Requests gear sheet | Apps → Timer → Approval threshold, shown only for a shared workspace whose member has `manage_time_tracking_requests` and `manage_workspace_settings`; the Requests gear opens it directly (`?focus=threshold`) |
| Mailbox settings, device reading and swipe actions | Mail gear (swipe actions only there) | Apps → Mail hub, now including swipe actions |
| Assistant personality, memory and on-device models | Mira menu | Apps → Assistant hub |
| Profile and account | Settings → You row and Edit profile | Settings → Edit profile (`/settings/profile`) |

Device-wide preferences stay at the root: Appearance and language, Time zone
(personal and workspace), and Notifications and interaction (reminders, haptics,
link browser). Tasks, Calendar, Finance and Timer render as routed sub-screens in
the shell with one top title, dock Back and dock-clearing scroll. Offline module
pages link to the app sub-screen instead of repeating its editors. The app
sub-screens link back to their offline data. Mail and Assistant keep their
account-scoped hubs, which an account or workspace switch cancels.

Summaries state the current value rather than repeating a title: Tasks reads
"Board view" or "Board list", Finance "Amounts shown" or "Amounts hidden", and
Calendar the effective first day. Each sub-screen group has a footer naming the
scope it changes: this device (Tasks board, Finance amounts, Pomodoro, offline
data), your account (first day of week) or the whole workspace (approval
threshold, calendar accounts). Calendar also links to the shared Time zone group
on the Settings root. The approval threshold editor uses grouped rows: a Require
approval switch, inline day and minute fields and a footer explaining both.

A threshold write is queued when offline and still reports success. The saved
threshold and grace period are therefore published into the cached workspace
settings that Timer and Requests read. A reload shows the saved value, not the
previous one, and live revalidation later replaces it with the server copy.
Overlapping saves apply in the order they were made.

In-app entry points use the same editor. The Timer gear and the Requests gear
deep-link to Apps → Timer. On return, Timer reloads Pomodoro lengths and Requests
reloads its threshold and permissions for the same workspace. The Mail gear
opens the current mailbox in the shared Mail editor. The Mira menu opens the
shared Assistant settings route at `/settings/apps/assistant`.

These stay contextual on purpose:

- quick toggles that edit the same stored value, such as the Finance eye
  action;
- the layout editors of their own surfaces, Home customize and Apps arrange;
- per-entity settings, such as a Meet room, a chat conversation's model and a
  habit tracker.

Routes and redirects:

- `/settings/apps` and unknown or hub-only app ids, such as
  `/settings/apps/mail`, redirect to `/settings`.
- `/tasks/settings`, `/calendar/settings`, `/finance/settings` and
  `/timer/settings` redirect to the matching sub-screen. They are registered
  ahead of the app registry's parameterised routes, so `/calendar/settings` is
  not read as the `/calendar/:eventId` detail. The app does not register a
  `/tasks/:taskId` route.
- Web and app links of the form `<workspace>/settings/apps/<app>` and
  `<workspace>/<app>/settings`, plus the Tasks host's `<workspace>/settings`,
  resolve to the same locations.
- `/settings/preferences` still redirects to `/settings`, and existing Settings
  routes are unchanged.

Settings search is the Settings root's primary floating dock action, not an
inline field. Opening it turns the shell's top navbar into the query field, and
results replace the root list, profile header included. Search matches titles,
sections and the settings on app sub-screens, ignoring case and Vietnamese
diacritics, including decomposed input with combining marks. A result for a
routed destination opens it. A result for an inline root row clears and closes
the search and scrolls that row's group into view. Isolated previews without the
shell chrome registry keep an inline field as a fallback. Settings and Offline
search retain the floating dock above the keyboard; dock and system Back clear
and close search before leaving the destination.

### Mail settings offline behavior

Mail account rows and mailbox settings restore an encrypted account/workspace
snapshot before background revalidation. Long addresses wrap within their row.
Save belongs to the floating navbar, and unsynchronized changes remain visibly
queued or require review until acknowledged by the server.

Cached mailbox roles never grant editing. Restored settings open immediately in
read-only mode until current-session membership can be verified. A previously
verified editor can queue an offline edit; an uncertain send retains its original
operation identity for manual review. Before a queued settings PATCH leaves the
device, its manual-review state is persisted. An interruption therefore cannot
automatically resend it after restart; an interruption before actual transmission
also conservatively requires review because the API has no operation receipt.
Settings use a separate outbox scope,
including legacy retained records, so a held settings write does not hold inbox
actions. Saves send changed top-level fields only; nested automation and group
policies remain complete objects. A sender-name edit preserves an unrelated web
signature change, but concurrent edits to the same field or policy still use the
server's existing last-write-wins contract. No revision precondition is claimed.
Server-setting controls stay read-only while that mailbox has an unresolved
edit, including when reopened, and until a fresh response installs the next
confirmed baseline. Retry after a mailbox denial requires a fresh owner/admin
management grant; cached roles never restore editing. Failed
edits do not overlay confirmed settings. A mailbox settings 403/404 removes only
that mailbox's snapshot; workspace bootstrap denial still revokes workspace Mail.
Authorization denial removes the affected snapshot;
a verification challenge retains it without granting new management access.
Refreshes preserve dirty form values. This behavior is covered by
`mail_settings_cache_ui_regression_test.dart` and
`mail_settings_offline_regression_test.dart`. Restart and in-flight Retry fencing
are covered by `mail_settings_dispatch_restart_test.dart`.

## Native text editing and keyboard dismissal

Across mobile routes and modal forms, editable fields keep their native copy,
cut, paste and select-all actions while the keyboard is visible. Tapping a
selection toolbar, selection handle or a `TextFieldTapRegion` accessory keeps
editing focus. A genuine outside tap dismisses the visible keyboard through
Flutter's `EditableTextTapOutsideIntent`. Dialogs that consume the inherited
keyboard inset still recognize the visible keyboard from the view. A field's
explicit `onTapOutside`
callback retains control of its behavior. The task interaction dismissal guard
still suspends outside dismissal. Without a visible keyboard, the existing
focus policy is delegated to Flutter, including mouse and stylus outside
dismissal.

Shadcn text fields and areas explicitly pair the platform menu builder with
native handle-only selection controls. Android and iOS retain draggable
selection handles; desktop keeps its platform handle policy. The device-name
and QR-link inputs use the same native editing controls. Workspace search
already uses a native Material field.

This applies to Material fields and shadcn fields and text areas, including the
Assistant composer and settings forms. Disabled fields remain disabled,
read-only fields allow selection and copying without mutation, and obscured
fields retain Flutter's restrictions on copying and cutting. Input formatters
and IME composition remain owned by the field.

Regression coverage lives in `test/app/view/text_editing_*_test.dart` and

Widget evidence does not replace native-device clipboard and IME verification.


## Private error diagnostics

Mobile Mira reply failures and timezone read/write failures offer Copy debug
information only to an authenticated account with a confirmed `@tuturuuu.com`
email. External addresses, unconfirmed addresses and MFA-required sessions do not
see this control. Email/confirmation changes update auth-state equality; logout
and account changes revoke the control and its stale callback.

Copying writes a local, fixed-field envelope to the clipboard: operation stage,
error category, HTTP status, retry delay and allowlisted rate-limit policy fields.
It never includes messages, prompts, response bodies, URLs, email addresses or
credentials, and never uploads diagnostics. Retry and cooldown rules are unchanged.
Recovery clears old diagnostics; timezone actor/workspace changes discard them.
Streamed HTTP errors retain at most 64 KiB before parsing. Error-body reads have
a total deadline matching the request timeout (30 seconds by default), even if
small chunks keep arriving. Deadline, source failure or overflow requests source
cancellation and discards partial payloads while preserving HTTP status and
headers for typed errors; transport cleanup cannot delay that result. Logout or
actor departure during this read cancels it and rejects publication, including
a return to the same actor. Successful streams keep their existing lifetime. Oversized bodies are
canceled and become generic status failures; status/retry headers survive.
Successful streams remain unread until consumed by the assistant transport.

Coverage: `safe_error_diagnostics_test.dart`, `api_client_stream_error_test.dart`,
`bounded_stream_error_test.dart`, `stream_error_deadline_test.dart`,
`internal_diagnostics_copy_test.dart` and
`timezone_safe_diagnostics_test.dart`. These tests establish local capture,
clipboard privacy and cooldown behavior; they do not establish the cause of a
production 429 or stopped reply.

## Handled timezone operational failures

Mobile timezone diagnostics distinguish preference load (`timezoneRead`), API
write (`timezoneWrite`) and unresolved native timezone after a successful write
(`timezoneResolve`). A genuine handled HTTP 429 records its status and bounded
Retry-After bucket once, while preserving the existing cooldown, failed edit and
explicit retry. A cooldown-blocked tap, logout, closed cubit or superseded ABA
request does not create an incident. Native lookup that is unnecessary for a
named preference also creates no incident.

The production Crashlytics boundary follows the fixed-field privacy and bounded
dedup defaults documented in [mobile chat](/platform/features/mobile-chat#handled-assistant-operational-failures).
It excludes timezone values and actor/workspace identifiers. Tests can inject a
sink without Firebase. Sink rejection leaves existing UI failure and retry
behavior intact. `timezone_operational_reporting_test.dart` exercises genuine
HTTP 429 responses through the authenticated API client and repository with
synthetic credentials, separate read/write phases, cooldown/recovery, stale
requests and sink failure. Native authenticated provider/device evidence remains
pending; this does not identify the cause of any customer's 429.

## Timezone pickers and sheet Back

The Personal timezone and Workspace timezone rows open a large sheet built on
the shared sheet host (`showAppSheet`). It shows every supported zone by city,
with its region and current GMT offset (for example, "Ho Chi Minh · Asia ·
GMT+7"). Zones are sorted west to east. The saved choice is pinned under
**Current** with a check mark. **Automatic** explains what it means:

- Personal: it follows the workspace timezone, or this device's when the
  workspace has none, and names the zone it resolves to now.
- Workspace: members without a personal timezone use their own device's.

Search is the floating dock's dynamic search action. It turns the shell's top
navbar into the query field, and the sheet has no inline search field. The query
matches city, region, IANA id or offset (`gmt+7`, `utc-5`, `+05:30`).

System Back, the dock Back and dragging the sheet down all close the picker
without saving, and the user stays on Settings. The same rule applies to every
sheet or dialog opened through `showAppSheet`, `showAppDialog` or
`showAdaptiveSheet`: Back closes the topmost overlay first and never pops the
page beneath. Saves, rate-limit cooldowns, permissions and the
account/workspace cancellation of an open editor are unchanged. These defaults
apply in English and Vietnamese, light and dark, and enlarged text. Coverage:
`settings_timezone_picker_checks.dart`, `app_sheet_host_test.dart` and
`timezone_choices_test.dart`.

Root preference rows still open nested editors and save only an explicit choice.
The no-root-switch decision recorded in
[`apps/mobile/docs/settings-calendar-timezone.md`](https://github.com/tutur3u/platform/blob/main/apps/mobile/docs/settings-calendar-timezone.md)
remains, so Settings shows no inline toggles at the root even though the visual
reference does. The Appearance row opens `/settings/appearance`. That is a child
page, and its controls apply and persist instantly because choosing there is the
explicit action. Visiting the page and returning writes no appearance preference.

Until a user first opens Appearance, its root row shows a "New" chip. Opening it
from that row features Aurora with a "New" chip in the gallery
(`/settings/appearance?highlight=aurora`). Any visit, including a deep link,
retires the chip through the `appearance-intro-seen` preference. Aurora is listed
first in the gallery; whether fresh installs should default to Aurora is an open
product decision.

### Appearance options and defaults

| Option | Choices | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mode | System, Light, Dark | System (an existing `theme-mode` preference is kept) |
| Theme | Aurora, Classic, Sunset, Ocean, Forest, Ember, Blossom, Mono | Classic |
| Accent | Match theme, Neutral, Indigo, Blue, Teal, Green, Amber, Orange, Rose, Violet | Match theme (Classic: Neutral) |
| Background | Solid, Gradient, Blobs (Vietnamese: Trơn, Dải màu, Đốm màu) | Solid |
| Intensity / Softness / Card opacity | Sliders; softness only for Blobs | 60% / 60% / 78% (min 40%) |
| Animate background | Blobs only, opt-in | Off |

Classic dark (Neutral accent, Solid background) returns the original shad and
Material themes. Classic light uses an iOS-style grouped page: a `#F2F2F7` page
with white cards and darker muted text (`#5E5E66`) that stays AA on the tinted
page. Gallery swatches always show each theme's gradient or blobs at full
intensity, so dark Classic, Mono and Ember remain distinguishable.
Picking a theme in the gallery also applies its recommended background style:
Aurora, Forest and Blossom use Blobs; Sunset, Ocean, Ember and Mono use Gradient;
Classic uses Solid. Reset to default restores every option, including System mode.

Curated colour values live only in `lib/core/theme/appearance/appearance_palettes.dart`.
`AppearanceTheme` derives the shad colour scheme and the Material bridge theme
from them; widgets use theme tokens. Foreground, muted, card and popover text keep
WCAG AA (4.5:1) against any blend of each theme's base, gradient, blob, card and
subtle-surface colours, in light and dark. Primary (accent) and destructive
colours are derived per theme: each is stepped toward black or white until it
reaches 4.5:1 against that worst-case blend. Labels on primary, secondary, accent
and muted fills, and Material `onError`/`onPrimary`, also reach 4.5:1. Non-neutral
accents also drive Material primary/secondary/tertiary containers and surface
tint, so chips, FABs and indicators follow the accent.

`AppearanceBackdrop` also installs `accessibleDestructiveButtonTheme` for every
appearance. shad's built-in destructive button paints a fixed white label on a
50% destructive fill (about 2.5:1 on light pages). The override uses the
theme's solid `destructive` fill with the `labelOn` label instead, so it reaches
4.5:1. Hover and press shift the fill slightly toward the label without losing
AA, and disabled buttons keep shad's styling. Default Classic dark uses the base
scheme, so its destructive buttons also change from a translucent to a solid
fill. Segmented-control labels (Mode, Background) scale down to fit rather than
truncate.

### App-wide application and cost

`AppearanceBackdrop` sits in the app builder around the router. For Gradient and
Blobs, it paints one ambient layer behind every route and makes shad scaffolds
transparent, so the theme shows across Settings, root tabs and detail pages. Its
child keeps a stable position, so changing styles never remounts navigation.
Imperatively pushed Material/Cupertino pages carry their own copy of the layer
beneath their content, so transitions never reveal the page below. The shell
header is clear on detail routes, so the gradient reaches the title and status
bar. On root tabs, where content scrolls under it, the header is frosted
(translucent background with an 18px backdrop blur). Solid themes keep the opaque
header. Adaptive
sheets, drawers and dialogs restore opaque scaffolds. Solid styles add no layer.

The layer uses radial and linear gradients, not image blur, inside a
`RepaintBoundary`, so its picture is recorded once. Impeller has no raster cache:
the GPU still redraws it every frame, which is cheap for gradients. Animated blobs are opt-in
and drift on a 48-second loop driven by a 15 fps timer. Only the painter repaints;
no widget above it rebuilds per tick. Motion stops when the system Reduce Motion
setting is on or tickers are disabled. The clock stops immediately when the app
leaves the foreground and resumes from the same phase.

#### Frosted glass on Android

Glass chrome (the dock material, the frosted root-tab header, adaptive sheets
and dialog scaffolds) goes through `GlassEffects` in
`lib/core/theme/appearance/glass_effects.dart`. A live `BackdropFilter`
re-reads and re-blurs the content behind it on every frame that content changes.
That is every scroll, sheet and transition frame. Each filter also splits the
GPU render pass. Android GPUs are tile-based and pay heavily for that, so on
Android (`defaultTargetPlatform`) the same surfaces draw without the blur. Their
fill closes 75% of the remaining gap to opaque: dock 0.72 becomes 0.93, header
0.55 becomes 0.89, and sheets and dialogs 0.88 become 0.97. iOS and desktop keep
the live blur. High contrast is always opaque with no blur. Shapes, borders,
shadows, colours and the ambient layer are unchanged on every platform.

Coverage: `glass_effects_test.dart` asserts the composited `BackdropFilterLayer`
count and fill per platform, and `adaptive_sheet_test.dart` checks both
platforms. The on-device frame benchmark
`integration_test/perf/glass_effects_benchmark_test.dart` compares blurred and
flat glass over Solid, Blobs and animated Blobs (`flutter drive --profile`; see
its header).

Persistence uses `SharedPreferences`: mode keeps the original `theme-mode` key and
customization is stored as versioned JSON under `appearance-v1`. Installs without
that key migrate to the default appearance with their saved mode. Unknown or
malformed values fall back field by field. Bootstrap preloads both values, so
cold start does not flash the default theme. A newer change or closing the cubit
fences older pending writes. Slider drags preview live and persist when released.

Coverage: `appearance_contrast_test.dart` checks every rendered text and role
pair (including destructive and Material `onError`) for every theme, background
style, brightness and accent, plus the defaults. `appearance_application_test.dart`
checks app-wide application without remounting, pushed-page layers, pixel output
for solid, gradient and blob styles, that reduced motion schedules no frames, and
that the blob clock stops in the background and resumes. `theme_appearance_test.dart`
covers migration, restart persistence, preview, write fencing, reset and decoding.
`settings_appearance_shell_checks.dart` runs the real shell for Classic
light/dark, Aurora blobs and Sunset gradient. It also covers instant
apply/persist/reset, reduced and full motion, the New chip and Aurora highlight,
the one-line workspace stat, 320px pages at 2x text in English and
Vietnamese, and screen-reader activation of theme tiles and accent swatches.
`appearance_root_tabs_render_test.dart` renders the Home and Apps root tabs in
Classic light and dark Aurora blobs. `settings_design_checks.dart` covers the row
grid. These are widget-level checks. Device visual, battery and frame-time
acceptance on the exact CI-built artifact remain separate gates.


### Custom controls and modal editors

Appearance, Storage and Offline use the shared design-system controls. Appearance
sliders preview immediately and persist on release. Storage opens as a large
hosted sheet; its clear confirmation remains disabled during an active download
and clears data only after explicit confirmation. Back dismisses the current
picker or cancels the confirmation before leaving Settings; outside dismissal
also cancels the confirmation.

Offline module search lives in the floating navbar. Browsing a stored namespace
opens a hosted sheet with its own navbar search and lazily rendered record details.
Search scans all locally retained records in that namespace, without fetching the
network. An open browser refreshes on a content revision; losing its owning actor,
workspace or permission dismisses it. Reopening starts a fresh search. The
standalone preview uses an inline search field when no shell is present.

Assistant quick settings and the central Assistant entry both open the routed
Assistant settings screen.
Nested personality and memory editors retain account/workspace cancellation and
write fences. System-owned license and permission screens keep their framework
or operating-system presentation.

`cache_storage_review_test.dart`, `offline_stored_items_test.dart`,
`offline_module_page_test.dart`, `offline_search_chrome_test.dart` and
