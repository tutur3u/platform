Canonical legal pages preserve each in-force document in full, including its footer.
Footer sentences mentioning draft review remain part of the published text until an
authorized revision replaces that document. Presentation adapters must not remove or
rewrite them. This applies to English and Vietnamese Next.js pages, the Worker/Start
candidate, and the retained TanStack display adapter; the latter runtime stays paused.

The `/legal/changes/full` page and its English and Vietnamese variants show proposals
with no effective date and with noindex metadata. The page begins with the notice that
updates are not yet in effect. Each proposed document keeps its own draft footer and
links to its currently effective page. One page heading contains document subheadings.
These pages do not change acceptance records or publish a newly effective agreement.

Regression coverage lives in `packages/legal/src/legal-revisions.test.ts` (historical
SHA-256 content and literal footer assertions), `apps/web/cloudflare/native-legal.test.tsx`
(canonical rendering), and `apps/web/cloudflare/legal-changes.test.tsx` (localized notices,
proposal footers, current-version links, headings, dates and indexing).

## What changed presentation and publishing gate

The EN/VI `/legal/changes` pages default to the approved Variant 1 notice that
updates are not yet in effect. `packages/legal/src/notice.ts` keeps `legalNotice`
unpublished with both dates unset. Only the release owner may enable the notice
and set N and N+30 after the user's explicit go. Enabling presentation does not
activate contracts or send email. Lex owns all legal wording; Maya owns brand and
contrast acceptance. The Lex fixture and snapshots retain both translations,
including the final AUP/community safety-restoration row. The separately owned
`legal-aup-community-restore` lane must land its matching clauses before notice
publication. Transition clauses and archived-version links also need Lex/release
acceptance before that later publication. Never introduce a language-prevails rule.

The binding notice starts at the top of the content and is never collapsible.
The notice's audience and timing list stays with it; the summary table and its
non-binding disclaimer share one neutral aside. No summary may replace clause
text. Internal lawyer/Lex review annotations are omitted from proposal presentation;
missing summaries render no placeholder. Proposed/not-yet-in-effect status and
draft-revision footers remain. In-force text and footers are unchanged. Proposal routes retain
noindex. This PR changes neither effective agreements nor acceptance records.

All source sections have stable IDs; the pure package diff matches those IDs and
retains exact sentences and whitespace. Redlines use strike-through removals,
underlined additions and localized screen-reader labels. Print uses expanded
inline redlines, link destinations and clause break protection. Mobile contents
and desktop sticky navigation share section anchors, retained by the EN/VI switch.
The scope uses white theme tokens, foreground headings/underlined links, Inter
with Noto Sans fallback, neutral summaries and AA status text. Brand blue is only
a navigation accent. The site chrome retains the official unmodified brand mark.

Regression coverage: `packages/legal/src/legal-diff.test.ts` checks Lex's fixture,
notice states, IDs and exact reconstruction; `apps/web/cloudflare/legal-redesign.test.tsx`
checks both locales/states/views, binding visibility, summary grouping, screen-reader
labels and print rules. The existing legal route tests keep indexing and full
proposed-text/footer coverage. Hosted builds, Maya's visual/contrast sign-off and
Lex's final publication acceptance remain separate gates.

### Unpublished state, publish gates and theming

While `legalNotice.published` is `false` the page is exactly the Variant 1 copy:
no Lex notice sentence (including the non-binding line) renders, the proposed
version block shows only "Proposed" and "Not yet in effect" (no version id, no
date), and no draft marker is shown. When published, the block shows the real
Terms version (without the internal draft suffix) and the notice date.

The change list and diff cover Terms, Privacy, DPA, SLA and Subprocessors only.
Acceptable Use and Community Guidelines are excluded because their in-force and
proposed clause ids do not pair, which would label every clause "Removed";
re-enable after the clause-restore work lands and Lex OKs the wording.

Publishing also requires `archiveLinks` per locale in the notice config; the
`{ARCHIVE_LINKS}` placeholder is never rendered literally and `getLegalNotice`
throws when the link text is unset. Lex approves the real archive URLs first.
The in-force footer decision (remove or keep) is still open; the proposed footers
must match it on publication day.

The scope uses the shared theme tokens directly (no local redefinition), so dark
theme works; the navbar brand mark switches by theme, and client controls receive
their strings as props instead of importing the message bundles.
