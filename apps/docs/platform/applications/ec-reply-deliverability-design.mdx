## Scope and status

Draft design for Easy Center daily and periodic reports and staff replies. All
examples are synthetic. This page proposes behavior; it does not enable receiving,
change senders, provision infrastructure, or apply a schema migration. Invitations
and paid custom-domain entitlements are separate designs.

Parents should reply to a report and reach authorized center staff, who can read,
assign, answer and resolve the conversation beside its student and parent records.
Undeliverable addresses must stop consuming sends. Defaults below require product,
security and privacy acceptance before activation. Preserve report approval,
unsubscribe, workspace permissions and unknown-send-outcome fences.

## Reply address and delivery flow

Keep the currently approved From identity until the project owner approves a
change. Configure one verified inbound subdomain, illustrated only as
`replies.example.test`, with a workspace routing policy. Verify DNS ownership, MX
routing and a synthetic ingress canary before readiness can become true. Sending
identities require SES verification and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment independently
of inbound MX verification. No DNS or sender change is authorized by this draft.

Proposed address: `r2.<opaque-id>.<mac>@replies.example.test`. Use a 128-bit random
identifier and a truncated 128-bit HMAC-SHA256, base64url encoded; the resulting
local part fits 64 bytes. MAC input is a canonical versioned tuple containing the
opaque ID, workspace, report/post generation, recipient binding, expiry and key
version, resolved from private storage. No student name, address or record ID
appears in the address. Constant-time verification, a versioned keyring, expiry
and revocation prevent tampering and support rotation. Store a lookup digest and
encrypted reconstruction material; never expose token values in logs or UI.

Extend the existing identity API with a versioned codec; do not silently change
its 48-hex parser. Retain legacy decoding only for legitimately issued identities
with their original scope and validity. Preserve immutable accepted/unknown send
history. Proposed token validity is 180 days; expired replies enter a bounded
staff-only exception queue without disclosing report contents or sending a bounce
loop. Validate this lifecycle against the eventual retention decision.

Cloudflare Email Routing hands raw mail to a Worker. The Worker verifies the exact
envelope domain and token, authenticates transport provenance, validates the
current workspace/record relationship, and admits durable work. Token possession
allows routing only: it does not authenticate a parent or authorize disclosure.
Require the bound parent address and verified sender-authentication evidence;
forwarded mail, changed guardians, ambiguous identity or failed authentication go
to restricted review. Never trust caller-supplied Authentication-Results headers.
A staff member must verify a changed contact through the existing contact process.

Use the token binding to select the workspace before matching `In-Reply-To` or
`References` against stored outbound/inbound Internet Message-IDs in that same
workspace and conversation. Store SES provider IDs separately from RFC IDs.
Conflicting token/header targets go to review; missing headers may start a thread
under the valid bound report. Subject matching alone must never join students or
workspaces. Deduplicate by transport event and raw digest, with collision review
for reused Message-IDs. Persist before acknowledging; failed persistence retries
within the configured budget. An acceptance timeout is reconciled, not resent.

## Staff inbox and safe replies

Provide an Easy Center Inbox within the workspace report/contact experience,
linked to the student, verified parent relationship, original report/post and
read-only report snapshot. Reuse mail storage but grant no access to the shared
notifications mailbox. Staff roles are viewer, responder, manager and suppression
administrator; membership alone is insufficient. Enforce workspace and record
permissions on reads, writes, search, attachment downloads and realtime events.

Support unassigned/mine/team queues, assign/reassign with optimistic concurrency,
reply drafts with approved templates and report context, and open/pending/resolved
states. Replies use the center's approved sender and the same protected send path;
never allow arbitrary From, recipient, CC or BCC fields to create an open relay.
Show send failures and suppression reasons, and preserve drafts. Accepted by SES
is distinct from delivered. New eligible parent replies reopen resolved threads.

Proposed SLA: first human response within one business day, using the workspace
timezone and staff calendar. Measure from the oldest unanswered eligible inbound
message, pause only for explicit waiting-on-parent state, and alert assigned staff
and then the manager once per escalation level. Auto-replies never satisfy SLA.
Provide unread, status, assignment, report period, student, overdue and delivery
filters; keep global suppression details hidden from ordinary workspace staff.

Detect `Auto-Submitted`, mailing-list/precedence signals, vacation headers, DSN MIME
and empty-envelope bounces. Classify OOO/auto-replies separately, avoid reopening
or generating automatic acknowledgements, and allow manual reclassification.
Only authenticated SES events may create infrastructure suppression; a forged
bounce-shaped email cannot blacklist an address. Human reports of abuse enter
staff review. Add bilingual reply instructions only after qualified routing:
“Reply to this email to reach Easy Center” and
“Trả lời email này để liên hệ Easy Center”.

Quarantine attachments before display or forwarding. Check MIME signatures,
allowlisted types, decompressed size, archive depth and malware verdict; reject
executables and password-protected archives unless a separate safe review process
exists. Default to 10 MB per message, 5 attachments, 20 MB expanded total and one
archive layer. A timeout or unavailable scanner stays quarantined. Use a private
object store, short-lived authorized downloads, sanitized HTML and blocked remote
images. No public scanner uploads of parent/student data. A compatible scanning
service and its processor agreement are rollout prerequisites.

## SES events and suppression

Configure a dedicated SES configuration set in `ap-southeast-1`, publishing send,
delivery, bounce, complaint and delivery-delay events to a Standard SNS topic.
Attach the set and opaque correlation tags on every relevant send, including raw
MIME staff replies. Limit SNS publish permission to the SES source account and
configuration set. AWS documents this [configuration-set destination](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/event-publishing-add-event-destination-sns.html).

SNS delivers to a Cloudflare HTTPS webhook. Verify the signature over AWS's
canonical envelope, require the expected topic/account/region, and validate the
certificate chain and exact HTTPS SNS certificate host/path with redirects
disabled. Verify subscription confirmations before confirming through a pinned
AWS endpoint; never fetch arbitrary SubscribeURL or certificate URLs. Prefer
signature version 2 and reject unconfigured versions. Follow AWS's
[SNS signature verification](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sns/latest/dg/sns-verify-signature-of-message.html).
Bound payloads, reject malformed events, deduplicate topic + SNS message ID, and
also deduplicate semantic delivery events by provider send/recipient/type.
Timestamp policy must accommodate documented retries; old unrecognized events
require reconciliation rather than silently restoring sending.

Resolve the trusted send ledger to workspace and recipient; event tags alone are
not authority. Atomically persist the event and hard-bounce/complaint suppression
before acknowledging success. Materialize suppression synchronously on this path;
queue expensive analysis afterwards. If persistence is unavailable, return a
retryable failure and halt affected outbound admission until event health recovers.
Unknown send IDs enter a restricted reconciliation queue and raise an alert.

| Event/state | Proposed transition |
| --- | --- |
| Permanent hard bounce or complaint | Immediate global address suppression plus workspace reason; no automatic expiry. Cancel unsent jobs. A workspace cannot override a global block. |
| Soft bounce, mailbox full or delay | Enter WATCHED; count one strike per distinct provider send and recipient, even if delay and bounce both arrive. Three distinct strikes in rolling 30 days create a workspace blacklist entry. |
| WATCHED below threshold | Hold for 24 hours after the latest transient event. Recheck DNS and authoritative suppression without sending a probe; permit at most one already-authorized due message per day after recheck. |
| WATCHED expiry | Expire strikes individually after 30 days; clear WATCHED only when no valid strikes remain and checks pass. Do not send on expiry. |
| Three-strike blacklist | Recheck after 30 days from last strike. Require staff-approved restore with recorded evidence before the next ordinary due send; no automatic test campaign. |
| Manual/global hard or complaint block | Remains until authorized audited restoration with address correction or renewed lawful permission as applicable, and SES-side reconciliation. A later delivery never clears it automatically. |

Apply conservative address normalization consistently (trim, domain normalization,
existing case policy); do not collapse plus tags or provider-specific dots.
Role-based and disposable addresses are WATCHED/review signals rather than blanket
rejections of legitimate guardian contacts. Aggregate domain failure signals with
minimum volume and a bounded hold; security operators may impose a global domain
block with evidence. One center's soft failures must not block unrelated centers.

Use a single authoritative pre-send decision at enqueue for feedback and again
immediately before provider invocation for races. Return `skipped` with reason
codes such as `workspace_blacklist`, `global_hard_bounce`, `complaint`,
`watch_cooldown`, `unsubscribed` or `suppression_unavailable`; distinguish retryable
holds from terminal skips. Suppression committed before admission wins; mail
already accepted by SES cannot be recalled. Serialize per-recipient admission
with suppression updates and minimize the remaining provider handoff window.
Never let a generic retry, test-send or manual reply bypass protection.

Import the existing manual `email_blacklist` idempotently using a private export
or server-side read, checkpoint and dry-run counts. Preserve email/domain/pattern
scope, provenance, reason and original block semantics; ambiguous patterns require
review and remain enforced by the legacy checker. Keep existing global blocks
global. Reconcile counts/checksums and concurrent writes before switching readers;
dual-read the union during transition, never use last-write-wins to clear blocks.
No customer export, address list or raw SNS payload belongs in a PR or log.

## Dashboard and alerts

Workspace staff see sent, accepted, delivered, skipped with reason, WATCHED,
blocked, unanswered and overdue counts. Operators see configuration-set/account
health, webhook lag, last verified event, queue age, DLQ, scanner failures and
remaining work budgets. Display time window and denominator with bounce/complaint
rates; use provider-send/recipient counts and exclude duplicates. Keep SES account
reputation metrics distinct from workspace application rates.

Proposed early warnings are 2% hard-bounce and 0.05% complaint rates (minimum 100
sends for rate warnings; any complaint still suppresses immediately), or 80% of
current SES quota. These are internal proposals, not claims about AWS limits.
Infrastructure and security on-call own alerts near account limits. Add a
five-minute stale-event warning while traffic exists, and deduplicate/escalate
alerts through existing operational channels. Audited restore shows actor, scope,
reason, evidence, previous state and result; ordinary staff see only actionable
workspace information, never another workspace's history.

## Cloudflare ownership and cost stop fences

Follow [Cloudflare backend ownership](/build/devops/cloudflare-backend-ownership):
Workers handle ingress and send admission; Queues isolate parsing/scanning and
outbound jobs; existing Cron Control handles bounded SLA, retention and recheck
batches. Supabase remains the system of record. Next routes provide authenticated
UI/API adapters only. Do not add Vercel background work, pg_cron, Edge Functions
or Trigger.dev processors, or run duplicate legacy and new queue owners.

Before pilot, approve workspace and global daily/monthly limits for sends,
accepted bytes, object storage, scan bytes, queue operations, database calls and
alerts. An unconfigured budget means disabled. Reserve worst-case units atomically
before work, including retry amplification; reconcile actual use without refunding
unknown outcomes. Initial qualification bounds: batches of 50, three attempts per
job within 24 hours, one scan per content hash/version, one SLA alert per escalation
level, finite DLQ retention, and operator-approved replay with its own budget.

Maintain a durable global/workspace stop state and monotonic epoch checked before
every paid operation, provider submission, retry, alarm and queue re-enqueue.
Recheck after leases or waits; restarting must not reset counters or stop state.
Completed, suppressed, expired, exhausted and cancelled jobs schedule no further
work. Stop outbound sends first; reserve a separate bounded budget for receiving
suppression events. If that budget is exhausted, keep sends stopped and persist
an explicit reconciliation gap. Automatic SNS/queue retries must have finite
policies; total shutdown disables upstream subscriptions/routing after durable
capture or explicitly recorded loss, not endless failure responses.

An application fence cannot eliminate unavoidable ingress invocations or bytes
already accepted by providers. Include an operator upstream-disable runbook and
measure those residual costs. Billing alerts, ingress rate limits and CPU caps
alone do not prove a spending cap. Before activation record service-specific
operation counts, failure/restart tests and stop evidence in the
[adoption guidance](/build/development-tools/oxc-cloudflare-adoption#runaway-work-and-cost-acceptance).

## Privacy and retention

Treat parent and minor correspondence, student links and suppression fingerprints
as personal data. Vietnam's [PDPL, Law 91/2025/QH15](https://chinhphu.vn/?classid=1&docid=214590&orggroupid=1&pageid=27160)
is effective from 1 January 2026. Privacy counsel must confirm applicable rules,
controller/processor roles, lawful basis, child/guardian requirements, notices,
rights handling and cross-border processing before pilot; this is a design gate,
not a legal determination. Permission to receive reports does not automatically
authorize marketing, AI training or unrelated reuse of replies.

Proposed configurable retention: raw MIME/quarantine 7 days, attachments 30 days,
conversation content 180 days after resolution, and minimized operational audit
365 days. These are product defaults for review, not statutory periods. Minimize
report snapshots; keep content out of telemetry and alert payloads. Encrypt content
and contact addresses; use keyed address digests for matching, with key rotation
and restricted access. Pseudonymized data remains protected.

Delete expired objects, derived text, search indexes and attachments with bounded
Cloudflare jobs; propagate deletion tombstones through retries and backups under
a documented backup-expiry policy. Existing immutable reply identities need a
reviewed retention-specific purge mechanism; never weaken send-outcome fences by
ordinary deletion/requeue. Retain only justified minimal suppression fingerprints
and reasons while necessary to prevent unwanted contact, review necessity annually,
and have counsel decide erasure exceptions and legal holds. Staff-only RLS and
record authorization apply to service RPCs too; test tenant isolation, removed
staff, changed guardians, signed-download expiry and access audits.

## Schema sketch only

Conceptual extensions, not executable SQL or a migration. Final names, constraints,
RLS and retention must be reviewed alongside the existing private mail schema.

| Entity | Proposed fields and invariants |
| --- | --- |
| Workspace mail policy | `ws_id`, verified inbound domain, approved sender reference, enabled/readiness state, SLA calendar, retention and budget policy versions. |
| Reply identity (extend existing) | versioned codec, opaque digest, MAC key version, encrypted reconstruction, expiry/revocation; immutable workspace, recipient and report/post generation. Unique generation binding, separate test/live identities; accepted/unknown cannot be reset. |
| Inbox conversation link | `ws_id`, existing mailbox/thread IDs, student ID, parent relationship ID, report-or-post reference, status, assignee, revision, oldest unanswered time, SLA due time. Composite tenant foreign keys; one context per thread. |
| Mail message/attachment (extend existing) | provider event ID, RFC Message-ID, parent headers, classification, dedupe digest, scan status/version, private object reference, retention deadline. Quarantine is default. |
| Delivery event | provider/account/region, event ID, send ID, recipient digest, workspace resolved from ledger, type, event/received times, redacted reason. Unique transport and semantic dedupe keys. |
| Suppression state and strikes | scope (`global` or workspace), address/domain/pattern digest, state, reason, source, expiry/recheck, restoration audit; unique explicit scope key (avoid nullable uniqueness loopholes). Strikes unique per send/recipient/scope. Hard/complaint has no automatic expiry. |
| Import checkpoint and audit | source row ID/version, import run, digest/count, cursor, actor, operation and outcome; preserve legacy reasons in restricted storage. |
| Work control | scope, stop epoch, budget period/reservations, job idempotency key, attempts, deadline, lease, terminal state and reconciliation status. |

All workspace relationships require matching tenant keys and checked current
permissions. Only infrastructure operators manage global state. Scoped staff APIs
return block decisions without leaking other tenants' events. Keep existing
blacklist/bounce functions as adapters during rollout; one policy evaluator owns
all sends, so old and new schemas cannot disagree permissively.

## Effort ranges and phased rollout

Ranges are incremental engineering dev-days for one experienced engineer per
slice, including implementation, focused tests and review fixes. They assume
reuse of the foundations above; DNS/provider approvals, legal review and calendar
waiting are excluded. Scanner integration and schema reconciliation drive the
upper bounds. Total sequential effort is **33–54 dev-days**, not elapsed time.

| Slice | Dev-days | Acceptance before advancing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Policy/schema and privacy contract | 3–5 | Approved sender/domain direction, parent binding, retention, isolation and migration plan. |
| B. SES event ingestion, suppression and manual import | 6–9 | Signed event forgery/replay tests, immediate hard/complaint block, three-strike boundaries and import reconciliation. |
| C. Signed reply routing and safe attachments | 7–12 | Versioned codec, durable ingress, threading, cross-tenant rejection, OOO/DSN filtering and scanner failure tests. |
| D. Staff inbox, assignments, replies and SLA | 8–12 | Authorized staff can complete a synthetic conversation; send and resolve races, permissions and bilingual UX pass. |
| E. Dashboard, alerts and cost fences | 4–7 | Correct denominators, audited restore, stop/restart and operation-count evidence. |
| F. Pilot, migration rehearsal and rollback | 5–9 | Exact-commit CI and Worker acceptance, bounded staged traffic, restore rehearsal and release-owner sign-off. |

1. **Foundation/shadow:** A then B, with policy simulation on synthetic events;
   preserve existing suppressions and reconcile the manual import. Production
   shadowing requires separate authorization and private telemetry.
2. **Suppression canary:** enforce B for one admitted workspace, initially behind
   explicit enablement. Land E's stop/budget controls before any billable pilot.
   Verify that queued sends skip immediately after accepted adverse events.
3. **Reply canary:** C and D on synthetic recipients; qualify both daily and
   periodic reports, token rotation/expiry, retries, scans and staff permissions.
   Only then enable Reply-To and instructional copy together.
4. **Controlled rollout:** F with user approval before real-parent mail. Expand
   workspace cohorts based on observed delivery and inbox handling, retaining
   per-workspace stop switches and an accountable staff owner.

Rollback disables new Reply-To issuance and outbound replies while retaining
already-issued address receiving for its validity/retention window. Keep all
suppression enforcement active. Cancel or quarantine unsent jobs; reconcile
accepted/unknown sends before any replay. Schema rollout is additive until
migration and retention rehearsals pass; do not drop historical identity tables.
If security requires stopping receiving too, use the upstream stop runbook and
record the gap for staff recovery rather than claiming replies remain available.

Required regression coverage also includes out-of-order delivery after complaint,
delay-plus-bounce dedupe, exactly three strikes at the 30-day boundary, concurrent
manual blacklist writes, no automatic expiry of hard blocks, removal of staff
access, private attachment URLs, deletion fencing, unknown SES acceptance, stop
during a lease and exhausted retry budgets. This draft executes none of those
runtime checks; implementation PRs must supply evidence before activation.

## Decisions to resolve before implementation/pilot

The project owner must approve the outbound display name/address (recommend keep
the existing approved identity initially), inbound subdomain/DNS owner, and real
parent pilot cohort. Product must accept SLA hours, WATCHED cooldown/restore rules
and domain-risk policy. Privacy/security must approve lawful basis, retention,
minor/guardian handling, scanner processor/location and global restoration
controls. Operations must approve numerical spend budgets, alert ownership and
stop procedures. Collect sender/domain approval once per project through the
program owner; no real mail or provider change is needed to review this design.
